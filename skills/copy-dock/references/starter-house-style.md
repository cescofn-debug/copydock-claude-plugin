# Starter house style

Use this only when Copy Dock isn't connected. It's a sensible default for product copy: plain, specific, calm and consistent. A team's own house style in Copy Dock always replaces it.

## Voice

1. **Plain words.** Use the word a reader would say out loud. "Use", not "utilise". "Start", not "initiate". "Sign in", not "authenticate".
2. **Specific, not vague.** Name the thing and the number. "3 files didn't upload", not "Some items failed".
3. **Reader first.** Lead with what matters to them, then the detail. Talk to "you"; use "we" only for what the product did.
4. **Calm.** No blame, no alarm, no jokes when something went wrong. Save warmth for good moments.
5. **No hype.** Cut "simply", "just", "easily", "seamless", "powerful", "revolutionary", "best-in-class" and "world-class". Show the benefit instead.
6. **Short.** One idea per sentence. Most UI sentences fit in 15 words. Cut words that don't change the meaning.
7. **Active and direct.** "We couldn't save your changes", not "Your changes could not be saved". Contractions are fine.

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for buttons, headings, labels and menu items: "Create project", not "Create Project".
- **Buttons are verbs** that say exactly what happens: "Delete project", "Send invite". Avoid "OK", "Submit", "Yes" and "Click here".
- **Same word for the same thing, everywhere.** If it's "workspace" in one place, it's never "team" or "space" elsewhere.
- **Numbers as digits** ("3 days"), dates written out unambiguously ("12 Mar 2026" or "March 12, 2026", matching what the product already uses).
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one, only for real wins. Never in errors or warnings.
- **No ALL CAPS** for emphasis. No "please" in error messages; no "sorry" unless the product truly failed the person.
- **Full stops** on sentences in body text and messages; none on buttons, labels, headings or single-phrase tooltips.
- **Placeholders** like Lorem ipsum, "TBD", "TODO" or "[copy here]" never ship.
- **Variables** such as `{name}` or `{{count}}` stay exactly as they are, and plurals are handled ("1 file", "2 files").

## Inclusive and accessible

- Gender-neutral language: "they", "people", "team members".
- Don't use directional or sensory-only instructions ("click the green button on the left"); name the control.
- Link text says where it goes: "Read the privacy policy", not "here".
- Alt text describes what the image shows and why it's there, in one sentence.
- Aim for a reading level most adults read easily (about grade 8 or lower).

## Self-review checklist

Before you hand over copy, check each line:

- [ ] Says what happened or what this is, and what to do next (where relevant)
- [ ] Plain words, no hype words, no jargon left unexplained
- [ ] Sentence case; buttons start with a verb
- [ ] Same terms as the rest of the product
- [ ] No exclamation marks in errors; no blame
- [ ] Fits the space (count characters when there's a limit)
- [ ] No placeholders; variables and plurals intact
- [ ] No facts, prices or claims you can't confirm
