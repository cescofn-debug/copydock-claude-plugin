# Notifications, toasts and emails

Notifications interrupt. Each one must earn the interruption by being useful at a glance.

## Toasts (short, temporary messages)

- **Successes say what changed**: "Saved. New drafts follow these settings." "Invite sent to ana@acme.com." Not just "Success!".
- Offer Undo when an action can be reversed: "Project archived. [Undo]".
- One line. No full stop if it's a fragment; full stops if it's sentences.
- Errors that need action don't belong in a toast that disappears; show them where the problem is.

## Push and in-app notifications

- **Lead with who and what:** "Sam asked you to review Checkout copy".
- Make the first 40 characters carry the meaning; the rest gets cut on lock screens.
- Specific, not generic: "Your export of 120 strings is ready", not "You have a new notification".
- No clickbait, no false urgency, no "Don't miss out!".

## Emails

- **Subject = the news or the action**, specific and under about 50 characters: "Sam asked for changes to checkout copy".
- **First line repeats the point**; many people read only the preview.
- One clear button for the main action; secondary links are plain text.
- Say why they got it and how to change that: "You get these when something needs you. Change how often."
- Transactional emails (receipts, sign-in links, password resets) stay plain and factual; say how long a link works.

## Examples

| Don't | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Success! | Changes saved |
| You have a new notification | Sam asked you to review "Payment declined" |
| Subject: Update from Acme!!! | Subject: Your invoice for March is ready |
| Action required | Your card expires on 31 March. Update it to keep your plan. |
