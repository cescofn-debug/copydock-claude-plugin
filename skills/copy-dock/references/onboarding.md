# Onboarding, tooltips and first-run help

People skim onboarding to get to their goal. Help them get there, and get out of the way.

## Onboarding steps

- **One idea per step**, tied to what the person wants to do, not a tour of features.
- **Headline = the benefit or the action:** "Invite your team", "Connect your first project".
- **One line of why**, then the action. Let people skip, and say how to come back: "You can do this later in Settings."
- **Progress is honest:** "Step 2 of 3". Don't promise "2 minutes" unless it's true.
- **Questions you ask** say why you ask and what changes: "What's your role? It sets what you see first."

## Tooltips and coach marks

- Explain what something does or why it matters, not what it's called. "Shows strings waiting for your review", not "Review queue".
- One or two short sentences, no heading unless it's a multi-step tour.
- Don't hide essential information in tooltips; if everyone needs it, put it on the page.

## Welcome and first-run messages

- Welcome briefly, then point to the first useful action. "Welcome to Acme. Start by creating your first project."
- Use the person's name only if they gave it.
- Celebrate real progress lightly: "Your first project is ready."

## Examples

| Don't | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome to the all-new, powerful Acme platform! | Welcome to Acme. Create your first project to get started. |
| Tooltip: "Review queue" | "Strings waiting for your approval" |
| Step 1: Settings | Step 1 of 3 · Invite your team. People who join see your house style straight away. |
